A sandbox for the NEAR hackathon idea: every memecoin launches with up to 3 rule cards. Try card combos, then read through who gets to change them and how they tie to NFTs.

Deck v2: memecoin speed

Effects run in minutes and hours, not days. Cards unlock as the coin grows. Cards are NFTs for one coin: you earn them, play them, and the card burns when played and turns into a Relic. The creator only sets the rules at launch; players hold and play the cards. All numbers come from deck-sim.js (tests A–K).

1 · Coin levels: cards unlock as the coin grows

Measured by real NEAR in the curve. The coin must stay above a level for 15 minutes to reach it, and a level never re-locks. Pumping to a level only costs about 2% in fees, and that money stays in the coin.

Every level-up drops cards. Only wallets that held for 1h before the level was crossed get one. Without that rule, a pumper's fresh buy ranked #1 and took the Epic drop (test J).

2 · Pacing: each tier runs on its own clock

With one shared meter, cheap cards drained it and the big cards never fired (test K). So each tier has its own limit, like abilities and an ult in Overwatch.

One day of play: about 61 Common, 45 Rare, 3 Epic, 2 Mythic. Even when a whale with a bot always plays first, the per-wallet cap cut its share of the big plays from 5/5 to 1/5.

5 · How players get cards

  • Card of the Day: one free Common for each wallet that checked in (minimum balance, held 1h+)
  • Level-up drops: top 1% by Conviction get an Epic, top 10% a Rare, everyone else eligible a Common (one per wallet per level)
  • Events: Hold the Line survivor gets a Rare · dip buyer a Common · contest winner an Epic · raid MVP a Rare · Prophecy hit a Rare
  • Sacrifice: burn tokens worth 0.05% of supply for a Common, 0.2% for a Rare, 1% for an Epic (6h hold needed), 3% for a Mythic (24h hold). Randomness only changes looks (art, 5% shiny)
  • Craft: 3 cards of one tier make 1 of the next tier (the 3 burn)
  • Trade: Rare and above can be sold on NFT markets. Commons can't be transferred, so bots have no reason to farm them
  • Hand limit 7: no hoarding

60 days with 500 holders gives 56% Common, 35% Rare, 7% Epic, 2% Mythic (test I).

If the card gets Warded, it still burns and you get no Relic. That's the risk of playing into a counter.

7 · Where NFTs fit

  • Card NFT (NEP-171): belongs to one coin, single use, Rare and above tradeable. A card for a popular coin is worth more.
  • Relic: can't be transferred, proves a play. Counts toward Evolution.
  • Badge: can't be transferred. Survivor, Dip Catcher, Raid MVP, Black Hole contributor, Cult Oath.
  • Mascot: one per coin. Levels up with the coin (Evolution); Rebrand changes its skin.

8 · Hard limits (contract-enforced)

  • Burns at most 5% of circulating supply per 24h; the pot spends at most 50% per 24h; sell fee at most 3%
  • One Epic or Mythic effect active at a time. All of it chained: +8.9% price capped vs +20.3% uncapped (test H)
  • No minting, no NEAR taken from the curve, no sell caps
  • Fuzz test: 0 states out of 1.2M where the coin couldn't pay everyone back. Steepen, Phoenix and reserve-funded Happy Hour broke that 500 out of 500 times; Flatten lowered the price every time (test A)

Cut: Phoenix, Steepen, Flatten, total buy/sell caps, Lottery Tax, Jackpot Block, random Wheel of Fate, random Blood Moon, reserve-funded Happy Hour, Heartbeat, Census. Later: Liquidity Rain.

    Picking Heartbeat's numbers

    % of total supply burned by day 60 for every threshold × burn pair (median of 10 runs, other settings as above). You want the left column near zero and the right column high: a healthy coin is left alone, a dying one shrinks.

    What the default run shows

    • Heartbeat tells healthy from dying. Threshold 40, burn 3%: a steady coin burns nothing, a whale exit about 2%, hype-then-fade about 8%, a slow death about 16%.
    • Burning from the fee pot barely moves supply. At most 0.4% in 60 days, because 1% fees on a small coin add up to only a few NEAR. Burning from the 200M reserve is what makes supply visibly react.
    • Keep the threshold about 20 points below a healthy pulse. At 60, a steady coin with 60% check-ins still loses 12% on noisy days.
    • Counting wallets lets bots hide a dying coin. 300 bots lift the pulse from 29% to 47%, and Heartbeat fires 4 times instead of 58. Counting tokens, they only reach 32%.
    • Rewards are small. A steady coin pays out about 6.6 NEAR in total over 60 days, so farming it with bots loses money.

    Sybil resistance, good enough for a demo

    You don't need proof-of-personhood. You need splitting into many wallets to be pointless, which takes three choices:

    • Count tokens, not wallets for the pulse, Streak shares and votes. 300 wallets holding 1M tokens count the same as one wallet holding 1M. To keep the coin "alive", someone has to own a real share of it and check in every day. That's a legitimate holder, not an attack.
    • Minimum balance + storage deposit + 3-day holding age before a wallet counts. This blocks dust-wallet spam and protects contract storage.
    • No per-head rules in the starter set. Census (holder count) and square-root voting both break under Sybil, so leave them out or label them as experimental.

    Say this plainly in the README: "Sybil-neutral by design: all weights are token-weighted. Not resistant to a whale; a whale keeping the pulse alive is buying the coin."

    The first 72 hours, hour by hour

    Traders on the bonding curve, each with their own rules. Snipers buy in the first seconds and sell at 2.5×, on a 20% drop, or within 6–15 hours. A whale buys 8 NEAR at hour 1 and dumps at hour 24. Momentum traders chase 15% moves and sell on a 15% drop. The community arrives with attention that fades, holds, and checks in. A farmer buys right before any buyback it can predict and sells right after. Each setup runs 30 times with random noise. Lines show the median, bands the middle 80%.

    What the default run shows

    • Snipers win on a plain curve. About +31 NEAR across 15 accounts, mostly taken from momentum traders. That is the launch problem, and no card changes it much.
    • A per-account cap works until snipers split wallets. It cuts sniper profit to about +10. With 75 accounts at 0.3 NEAR each, it is back to +35.
    • Locks backfire. Freezing early tokens removes sell pressure, so price squeezes to about 23× and then crashes 94% when they unlock. Snipers earn more (+43). Gradual vesting does the same, more slowly.
    • An early sell tax helps a little and can't be dodged. Starting at 10% and falling to 1% by hour 12, it cuts sniper profit about 20% (8% if they split wallets), more than doubles the fee pot, and raises community profit.
    • Scheduled buybacks get farmed. Drip buybacks don't. The farmer comes out ahead in about half the runs against a daily lump buyback, and in every run when big snipers swell the pot. Against drip it loses its fees every time.
    • Pulse Fee does nothing at launch. New buyers count as checked in, so the pulse stays around 60–65%. It is a card for weeks, not hours.
    • A flatter curve calms everything. At 90 virtual NEAR the peak drops from about 8× to 3× and the worst drop from 72% to 52%. Snipers still make about +24.

    What to build from this

    • Built in, not cards: drip buybacks only, the early sell tax, a capped creator first buy, and min-out on every trade.
    • Drop Phoenix and any buyback at a known time. Both are free money for front-runners.
    • Don't force-lock early buyers. If you want locking, make it opt-in with a reward such as a Streak bonus.
    • Keep Happy Hour as the launch-day attention card: about 8% more holders, cheap and hard to abuse.
    • Heartbeat and Pulse Fee are the slow cards. They tell the coin's story over days and weeks, not its first hours.
    • Say it in the README: snipers can't be fully stopped on a public curve. These rules shrink their edge and leave every other group better off.

    Four card types. Supply cards change how many tokens exist. Treasury cards decide where the fee pot goes. Ritual cards reward holders for doing something on-chain. Charm cards modify the other cards, which is where combos come from. One rule holds for every card: it can only touch the coin's treasury and tokens people chose to lock. It never pulls from a wallet and never changes the buy/sell curve.

    The question

    Cards are the coin's rules. If anyone can change rules whenever they want, nobody can trust the coin. If nobody can ever change them, there's no game. And with thousands of holders, "let holders decide" breaks quickly: whales outvote everyone, bots flash-buy to vote, and the creator can stack wallets. Five ways to set it up:

    My recommendation: Anchor slot + 2 council slots

    Slot 1 is the coin's identity. The creator sets it at launch and it never changes (model A), so a Heartbeat coin stays a Heartbeat coin. Slots 2 and 3 are chosen by holders (model C), and new cards come from the earned card NFTs (model E, if there's time). That gives judges one sharp hero mechanic plus a game that keeps going, and nobody can quietly rewrite the coin. Model D makes a strong pitch for the NFT track if you want a second angle.

    Stopping manipulation

    • Lock to vote, and the lock must already be a day old. Flash-buying the coin can't swing a vote.
    • Snapshot at proposal. Weight is taken when the proposal opens, so buying during a vote does nothing.
    • Square-root weight, capped at 5% per account. This softens whales. Splitting a bag across many wallets games square roots, though, which is why the cap and the streak multiplier matter: fake wallets don't check in every day.
    • Hard limits written into each card. A council can choose 1–5% burn and nothing outside that range.
    • One change per week, plus a 48h timelock. Holders who dislike a change have time to sell first.
    • Anyone can run the tick. No bot or creator decides when events fire; there's no trusted keeper.

    Vote weight calculator

    Proposed weight = √locked × streak bonus (up to 2× at a 30-day streak), and zero if the lock is less than 1 day old. Compare one whale against a crowd of small holders.

    A card's life

    Levels, kept small

    Each level widens the card's limits by one notch (Streak's pool range of 0.5–3% becomes 0.5–3.5%) and changes the art. A level never adds a new effect, so the safety review of each card stays true.

    Not gambling

    No paid packs and no random drops. Every card is earned by doing something specific. That keeps it out of loot-box territory and makes it easy to explain in the README.
