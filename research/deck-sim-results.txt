=== B. Inferno: what a pot burn does to price, and who can profit by trading ahead of it ===
Coin: 60 NEAR real in curve. Front-runner buys 3 NEAR before and sells after. Round trip fees = 2%.
pot spent | price lift | lump: front-run profit | drip 12 chunks: per-chunk sandwich profit | caster lock
   1 NEAR |   2.4% |   0.008 NEAR (0.3%) |  -0.054 NEAR (-1.8%) | caster must hold 24h
   2 NEAR |   4.9% |   0.076 NEAR (2.5%) |  -0.049 NEAR (-1.6%) | caster must hold 24h
   4 NEAR |   9.8% |   0.214 NEAR (7.1%) |  -0.037 NEAR (-1.2%) | caster must hold 24h
   8 NEAR |  20.0% |   0.497 NEAR (16.6%) |  -0.015 NEAR (-0.5%) | caster must hold 24h
Reading: a public lump Inferno is a free trade for whoever is fastest (bots). Dripped in chunks small enough that each
chunk moves price by less than the 2% round-trip fee, nobody can sandwich a chunk; the total lift is the same.
Buying on the announcement still profits, but that is open to everyone at once (it IS the hype), not to an insider.

=== C. Shield variants: global buy cap and global sell cap per 10-minute window (first hour of launch) ===
buy cap none : bots own 91.6% of hour-1 tokens, community orders filled 100.0%
buy cap 10 N : bots own 100.0% of hour-1 tokens, community orders filled 0.0%
buy cap 5 N  : bots own 100.0% of hour-1 tokens, community orders filled 0.0%
buy cap 3 N  : bots own 100.0% of hour-1 tokens, community orders filled 0.0%
sell cap 3% of supply / 10 min: whale finishes dumping in window 7; ordinary holders cannot sell at all until window 9+ (90 min locked in a crash).
Reading: a global buy cap hands the whole cap to whoever is fastest each window, so bots win harder. A global sell cap is a honeypot.

=== D. Happy Hour paid by the pot: +10% bonus tokens on buys for 30 min (vs the same trades with no Happy Hour) ===
bonus paid instantly : extra price lift vs no HH 3.6%, farmers' gain vs no HH 1.211 NEAR on 10 NEAR flipped
bonus vests 72h      : extra price lift vs no HH 3.6%, farmers' gain vs no HH 0.241 NEAR on 10 NEAR flipped
Reading: the pot buying bonus tokens adds a little real buy pressure. Paid instantly, flippers pocket the bonus;
vested (sell early = bonus burned), flipping earns only the small pot-driven lift and the forfeits burn, which lifts the floor.

=== E. Hold the Line + Dip Catcher: can a whale farm it with two wallets? ===
panic  0% of others sell: drop from dump 49.0% (triggers at 20%), prize to wallet B 0.074 NEAR, dump round-trip cost 0.622 NEAR -> loses money
panic  5% of others sell: drop from dump 49.0% (triggers at 20%), prize to wallet B 0.086 NEAR, dump round-trip cost 0.622 NEAR -> loses money
panic 15% of others sell: drop from dump 49.0% (triggers at 20%), prize to wallet B 0.109 NEAR, dump round-trip cost 0.622 NEAR -> loses money
Reading: the wallet-B prize stays well below what the dump costs in fees and slippage, so farming it loses money.
Rules that keep it so: eligibility snapshot taken BEFORE the drop, 1h holding age, prize = tolls + at most 20% of pot.
Note: a whale can still dump first and rebuy after a panic; that is ordinary market risk and exists with or without the card.

=== F. Blood Moon as a minority game (pick Red or Blue in secret; smaller side by tokens wins, 20% of losers' stakes burn) ===
whale sit out         : expected return   0.0% of stake
whale all on one side : expected return -99.3% of stake
whale split 50/50     : expected return -1.3% of stake
ordinary player (no whale): expected return -5.4% of stake (the 20% burn, shared)
Reading: a whale that joins one side makes it the majority and loses; splitting just pays the burn. Size cannot buy a win here.

=== G. Raid tactics: Rally / Curse / Ward each round. Does any one dominate? ===
first guess Rally +20%, Curse -15%, Ward reflect -5% -> best mix: Rally 16%, Curse 64%, Ward 21%
all 10%     Rally +10%, Curse -10%, Ward reflect -10% -> best mix: Rally 39%, Curse 39%, Ward 22%
tuned       Rally +7%, Curse -10%, Ward reflect -7% -> best mix: Rally 34%, Curse 34%, Ward 33%
Max swing per round from a perfect read: 7.0% of score. Reading the opponent right in 2 of 4 rounds closes a money gap of about 14%.
Reading: with the first guess, Curse is played twice as often as anything else (overpowered). Tuned, it is a fair
rock-paper-scissors: no card dominates, smart play beats a ~15% bigger wallet, but money still beats bad reads.
Mirror (copy last round's card) beats opponents who repeat themselves and loses to ones who adapt; it stays a Rare tech card.

=== J. Market-cap levels: can someone pump the coin to unlock big cards or farm the level-up card drop? ===
Levels (real NEAR in curve): L1 0N=Common, L2 5N=Rare, L3 20N=Epic, L4 60N=Mythic, L5 120N=Graduation
pump 3N -> 20N: round trip costs 0.342 NEAR (2.0%). Without an age rule the pumper ranks #1/201 by Conviction -> wins an Epic drop. With "held 1h before the level": no drop.
pump 10N -> 60N: round trip costs 1.005 NEAR (2.0%). Without an age rule the pumper ranks #1/201 by Conviction -> wins an Epic drop. With "held 1h before the level": no drop.
Reading: pumping to a level only costs fees, and that money stays in the coin, so unlocking that way is fine. The level-up
drop is what needs protecting: only wallets that held 1h BEFORE the level was crossed get drops, ranked by Conviction.

=== K. Casting for 24h: a whale with 20 cards and a bot (always first) vs 150 holders with 1-2 cards each ===
big cards first-come       : 111 casts (Common 61, Rare 45, Epic 3, Mythic 2); whale made 18.0% of casts, 5/5 of the Epic+Mythic
1 Epic+ per wallet per 24h : 111 casts (Common 61, Rare 45, Epic 3, Mythic 2); whale made 14.4% of casts, 1/5 of the Epic+Mythic
Reading: separate pacing per tier keeps all tiers alive (no tier starves another): ~1 Rare every 15m, an Epic every few
hours, 2 Mythic Eclipses a day. The per-wallet daily cap on big casts stops one whale+bot from owning every big moment.
